## Today's Update

Thirty-four units across thirteen feature areas. That number sounds like chaos, but the day had a discernible shape: close out the state machine remediation evidence review, then work through a cluster of correctness concerns in bookings and decisions that had been accumulating while the heavier architectural work was running.

The state machine remediation thread finally reached its evidence review phase. I worked through the remaining agent/browser gate acceptance criteria, reviewed the finance Gate 1 evidence, the appointment replay Gate 1 evidence, the current-authority async evidence, and the native browser recovery evidence. This was less about writing new code and more about holding what's already been built up to the gate criteria and confirming it passes. The distinction matters because passing an evidence gate at this stage is a commitment — future gate logic won't re-litigate these contracts unless something in the underlying mechanics changes. I'm reasonably confident in the finance and replay proofs. The current-authority async evidence is the one I'd want to revisit if the async dispatch path gets refactored; the claim-execution-recovery sequence from last week is stable, but async authority reads have a way of masking timing assumptions that only surface under concurrent load.

The bookings work was the densest cluster of the day. Nine items, not because I was grinding through a queue but because each one exposed the next one. Aligning new admission-basis cases with the actual suppression path the system records meant reading what the path produces rather than what I intended it to produce — those two things agreed in most cases but not all. From there: isolating ineligible notice fixture keys so test data doesn't bleed across cases, restoring the reminder customer-status assertions that a merge had silently dropped, dropping duplicated case-count lines that the develop merge introduced, and rejecting unpersisted cancellation referrals at the boundary rather than letting them propagate. The most consequential addition was recording why a held notice is held and surfacing that reason to authorized operators. Right now that field is empty in any held state — future operators debugging a stalled notice queue would have no signal about whether the hold was intentional, transient, or a defect. Writing the reason at hold time makes that distinction traceable. The finance HTTP proofs getting qualified on MySQL closes a gap I'd been carrying since those proofs were written against SQLite defaults; the behavior isn't identical between engines on certain constraint orderings, and I'd rather find out now than when the finance path is exercised in a real tenant workflow. Last in the bookings sequence: writing refusal history atomically with clearing events and stale governance cleanup. The atomicity is the load-bearing part — a refusal history written outside the transaction that also clears the governance record could leave the system in a state where the history says one thing and the governance state says another.

The decisions and trust work followed from the bookings changes. Keeping published reminders in a pending state until settlement, fencing outbox settlement by active attempt, and fencing reminder settlement by current authority are three versions of the same architectural instinct: don't let a settlement happen against stale preconditions. The outbox fence is the strictest of these — it ties settlement eligibility to whether an active attempt actually exists, which means a lost or expired attempt can't accidentally trigger a settlement that wasn't earned. On the trust side, recording the admission basis on the policy decision established by the engine (rather than re-deriving it at read time) closes a representational gap: the engine has authority over what basis was actually applied, and carrying that through to the policy decision record means downstream readers don't have to reconstruct it. That reconstruction would be correct most of the time, but "most of the time" is the wrong standard for a compliance record.

The remaining items — asymmetric receipt signing and evidence retention in the external agent surface preconditions, attaching typed refusal reasons in ship-privacy-comms, intelligence reconciliation for today's briefing, and the v3 reader alignment with the compact trace byte limit — were individually smaller but each clears a specific ambiguity. The asymmetric receipt signing is the one I'd call out: unsigned receipts in the evidence store are functionally fine for now, but adding signing before the external agent surface gets exercised is substantially cheaper than retrofitting it afterward, when the receipt format will have become an implicit contract with whatever consumes it.

Tomorrow the appointment gap closure policy reconciliation item (the D13-A delivery versus mandatory Trust review question) is the one I want to think through before touching code. The automatic delivery path and the mandatory review path aren't inherently in conflict, but the sequencing of when the Trust gate fires relative to delivery timing is a design decision I haven't fully committed to yet.
