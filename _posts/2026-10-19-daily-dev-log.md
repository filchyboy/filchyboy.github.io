## Today's Update

Thirty-five work units across fourteen feature areas, but the day wasn't actually scattered — it had a spine. The common thread was evidence integrity: establishing what counts as proof, who can read it, and what happens when a record needs to survive a transition without losing its provenance.

The state machine remediation work closed out its remaining gate reviews. I recorded the final agent/browser acceptance criteria, then worked through the Gate 1 evidence for finance, appointment replay, current-authority async, and native browser recovery. These aren't new implementation — they're the verification layer asserting that what was built against the spec actually satisfies it. Getting these recorded now matters because the gate criteria govern what can advance past the current checkpoint; having ambiguous or missing evidence at that layer creates exactly the kind of decision debt that compounds. The bookings feature set was the heaviest lift of the day across raw item count. I aligned the new admission-basis cases with the suppression paths the code actually records (there was a gap between what the plan described and what the path produces), isolated ineligible notice fixture keys so test data doesn't bleed across cases, restored reminder customer-status assertions that had gone missing after a develop merge, and dropped the duplicated case-count lines that same merge introduced. The more structural changes were: rejecting unpersisted cancellation referrals at the boundary rather than letting them fail silently downstream, recording why a held notice is held and surfacing that to authorized operators, and writing refusal history atomically with clearing events and stale governance. That last one is the one I care most about. Atomic writes here aren't just a correctness concern — they're the difference between a refusal history that can be audited and one that's only partially reliable because a mid-write failure left it in an indeterminate state.

The external-agent-surface-preconditions work involved two related pieces: completing receipt fields on existing evidence stores, then implementing asymmetric receipt signing and fixing evidence retention. The signing is the load-bearing part. An unsigned receipt can be fabricated or corrupted silently; an asymmetrically-signed one ties the evidence to a specific issuance event in a way that survives whatever handling happens afterward. The retention fix matters because signing is only useful if the signed artifact is actually kept — and there was a path that wasn't retaining it correctly. The decisions feature set got its own cluster of correctness work: restoring native authority fixture contracts, keeping published reminders pending rather than letting them settle prematurely, and fencing outbox settlement by active attempt and reminder settlement by current authority. The two fences are related — both prevent a settlement from completing against an authority state that has since changed. The difference is surface: outbox settlement sees the attempt context, reminder settlement sees the authority context. Getting both fences explicit in the contract means the implementation can't inadvertently rely on ordering assumptions that won't hold under concurrent load.

The trust, compliance, and build work filled in the remaining seams. Recording the admission basis on the policy decision (established by the engine, not inferred at read time) is the kind of thing that's invisible when it's right and very visible when it's wrong — a policy decision that doesn't carry its own admission basis requires the reader to reconstruct that context from somewhere else, which breaks the moment state diverges. The build work aligned the v3 reader with the compact trace byte limit and bound retained epoch metadata to the final inventory, both of which are preconditions for the build artifact to be self-consistent. The intelligence reconciliation items closed the October 19 daily briefing loop: reconciled, finalized the execution receipt, recorded the owner-reviewed boundaries, and produced the delivery receipt. That sequence is procedurally straightforward but has to happen in order — you can't finalize the receipt before the reconciliation is complete, and you can't record the delivery receipt before the execution receipt exists.

The appointment-gap-closure policy reconciliation — specifically D13-A, reconciling automatic delivery with mandatory Trust review — is worth flagging as a design question still open in my head. The policy decision is recorded, but the interaction between "automatic" and "mandatory review" depends on sequencing that isn't fully locked down yet. Whether Trust review can gate automatic delivery or only annotate it after the fact is a real question, and the answer will determine whether that path is safe to exercise before the review mechanism is complete. That's the one piece from today where I'm not certain the recorded decision is the final one.
