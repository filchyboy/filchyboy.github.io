## Today's Update

The throughline today was privacy infrastructure — not any single feature, but the set of contracts that every privacy-adjacent workflow will eventually depend on: category schemes, version identities, immutability proofs, and cycle rejection. A lot of items completed, but most of them are variations on one underlying design question: what does it mean for a privacy category to be stable, and how does the system enforce that stability at every layer that touches it?

The compliance and privacy feature sets did the heaviest lifting here. On the compliance side, I worked through scheme metadata persistence, the integrity checks on top of it, and the policy gating that controls whether a category draft can progress at all. The sequence mattered: scheme metadata has to be immutable before you can write meaningful policy checks against it, and policy checks have to exist before activation gating means anything. Rebuilding the owner resolver after the test adapter tag change was also in this cluster — that's the kind of fix that's invisible until a test suite starts failing in confusing ways, and getting it done before the fixture base grows further saves a lot of confusion. The privacy side covered the same territory from a different angle: binding scheme versions to stable identities, rejecting cycles in stored catalog payloads, isolating principal audiences while retaining the canonical privacy entry. The catalog cycle rejection is worth calling out specifically — a catalog with a reference cycle will deadlock any traversal that doesn't explicitly detect it, and discovering that at enforcement time rather than at read time is the correct place to surface it.

The DSR work was the largest single cluster — twelve items covering the full arc from subject export selection through package verification and fulfillment. The export path now enforces governed byte verification before settlement, reads the package back from local disk to confirm it's actually there, and validates the fulfillment route ID via a form request rather than trusting the caller. Separately, the UI states around stale exports got cleaned up: a stale download suppresses on status error, and a failed status clears the success state rather than leaving both signals asserted simultaneously. That last one is a subtle correctness issue — having both success and error states present in the UI at once isn't an edge case, it's what happens any time the status poll runs after a successful download if you don't explicitly clear it. The decisions work ran alongside this: reminder admission lock races are now qualified, outbox claims are fenced against stale entries, and reminder claims are bound to the original reviewer with tenancy rechecked. That binding matters because a reminder that migrates to a different reviewer loses the original approval context, and the tenancy recheck prevents a claim from being exercised against a different tenant's case.

The email work finalized the Postmark-only transactional delivery boundary — D15 in the appointment gap closure plan. That constraint is actually not trivial to enforce, because the places where delivery decisions are made are not all in one location. Getting a proper boundary in place means future delivery code can't accidentally route transactional mail through an alternate provider without explicitly overriding a hard gate. The purpose-market evidence checks for recommendations in both commerce and email are related: the evidence requirements are enforced at the provider boundary, not just at the caller, so a recommendation without the right purpose-market pairing is refused before it can produce a delivery attempt. Whether those two enforcement points are redundant or genuinely complementary is something I'll want to revisit — right now they feel like defense-in-depth, but I want to make sure neither one becomes the point where the other's enforcement gets silently assumed away.

The scheme successor publication and governed DSR export execution items are both still in progress. The successor publication work is the piece I'm least settled on right now — stable scheme identities are done, but the publication contract for successor schemes has some open questions about what constitutes a valid predecessor reference and how that's verified at publication time rather than only at read time. That's the thread to resolve tomorrow before the scheme infrastructure work closes.
