## Today's Update

Today was effectively a platform-wide ledger day — the kind where the breadth of what moved is harder to summarize than the depth of any single piece. The dominant story is the Operational Escalation feature, which went from blank schema files to a fully gated, documented system in a single session. That's not a small thing. The feature covers the entire lifecycle: taxonomy enums (severity, category, blocking posture, lifecycle state, delivery status, principal type, acceptance outcomes), the five-table schema (aggregate, lifecycle events, source references, outbox, quota buckets), factories for all of them, the admission action with idempotency and quota locking, the MCP route and controller, the background workload adapter, the delivery outbox with claim/retry/reconciliation logic, retention scheduling with keyed tombstone verification, and the Admin projection pair with its own delivery-key migration. Then tests for all of it — including MySQL 8 concurrency proofs for replay races, quota races, and delivery integrity. The fail-closed configuration default was a deliberate choice I kept returning to: when quota state is ambiguous, the system refuses rather than permits, which is the only defensible posture before this path carries real operational weight. I'm reasonably confident the delivery retry accounting is correct, but the reconciliation deadline logic is the one area I'd want a second read on before this is exercised at any volume.

The capability-governance feature was the other major undertaking — 85 units covering the full semantic defense stack from ADR-0334 through P8 closeout. The architecture here is genuinely complex: canonical semantic object types (Effect, StateSurface, effect-rule) with maker-checker admission, immutable versioned rules, the TOON capability-to-effect mapping and projection compiler, AgentRuntime environment attestation with fingerprint canonicalization and drift detection, and then the TrustFabric V2 policy decision layer implementing C0 through C4 classification tiers with dual-dispatch shadow mode. The C4 authorization path — issuance and atomic redemption — is probably the most sensitive piece, because an error there either blocks legitimate execution or permits unauthorized capability access. The dual-dispatch approach (V1 controlling, V2 shadow) is the right way to validate V2 behavior before switching control, and the scoped rollout admission gate means that switch can be made incrementally per environment rather than as a global flag flip. I also built the full defense state machinery: posture epochs, effect-level revocation, emergency revocation with time-boxing, containment case lifecycle with automatic ceiling enforcement, and C2 automatic recovery versus explicit C3/C4 lift paths. The operator surface — APIs, CLI, reports, metrics, runbooks — came at the end of P7 and represents the controls future platform-security principals will actually use day-to-day.

Alongside those two large features, I completed ten horizontal slices and ten thin vertical slices. The horizontal slices standardized patterns across the platform: HS186 locked BillingAccount identity into the routing event and made `RouteDecisionIntentJob` tenant-aware with lifecycle-version idempotency on retries; HS197 moved billing providers, the QuickBooks adapter, Shopify, and Phrase through a unified outbound resilience interface; HS198 migrated 27 form requests (Cognitive reads, Cognitive mutations, Intervention requests) to a governed tenant-request interface; HS199 converged Workspace API response envelopes across approval inbox and read controllers; HS200 migrated 19 frontend calls (targeting, analytics, loyalty, CDP provenance) to a single transport contract; HS196 standardized four publication queue jobs (moderation, feed, cache, syndication) on a typed payload interface; HS201 structured privacy lifecycle logging across 22 events with redaction tests; HS204 migrated billing summary, billing account usage, and booking surfaces to a locale formatter contract; HS203 converged loading and empty-state primitives across Collision, Page Editor, and KPI surfaces; and HS205 added GPC platform-scope telemetry with two-account isolation and synthetic replay safety. The thin vertical slices — covering cost dashboard PDF export, documentation feature coverage KPIs, analytics SNS alert delivery, logging API performance metrics, billing anomaly email delivery, Mailchimp webhook history, compliance Slack alerts, retention cohort generation, monitoring availability SLOs, and price-change jurisdiction requirements — each went through the full eight-step sequence from contract scope baseline through quality gates handoff. I'm genuinely uncertain whether some of the observability instrumentation units for the thinner slices are as complete as they should be, or whether I traded depth for coverage there. That's worth reviewing before any of those delivery paths are exercised.

The Third-Party AI Content Publication Admission feature landed 17 units covering the admission vocabulary and versioned value objects, provenance persistence for revision declarations and attestations, authorship derivation with conflict evidence, typed disclosure applicability profiles per destination, the synchronous idempotent admission interface, Publication Channel declaration and attestation workflow, PageEditor review snapshot binding, media governance, accessible disclosure rendering with machine-readable marks, rendering evidence return to Provenance, tenant disclosure policy controls with impact preview, scoped exceptions, corrections and supersession with retention and privacy disposition, and reconciliation controls. The Article 50 candidates were encoded as non-blocking draft profiles — the right call given their regulatory status is still evolving. Beyond those larger features, the Market Intelligence Research System got the Stage 2 sampling frame approved and the source registry populated, which unblocks the next research phase. The data-sanitization, admin, MCP, and decisions modules each received targeted additions — bounded inline payload sanitizer, idempotent escalation projections, governed escalation admission exposure, and routing hardening after review feedback.

What today's volume actually establishes is a platform where the trust, governance, and escalation layers are no longer stubs. The capability governance stack defines how future tool invocations are classified and controlled. The operational escalation system defines how platform-level failures are surfaced and delivered to administrators. The form request, transport, envelope, queue payload, locale formatting, and UI state slices reduce the surface area of inconsistency that would otherwise accumulate into coordination debt. The thin vertical slices ensure that concrete delivery paths — PDF export, SNS alerts, webhook history, SLO tracking — have defined contracts before any of them are wired to real infrastructure. The honest assessment: the breadth was necessary, and the quality gates ran green, but the areas I'd revisit first are the delivery reconciliation deadline logic in escalation and the observability completeness in the thinner vertical slices.
