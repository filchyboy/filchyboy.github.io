## Today's Update

Today was a volume day — not in the sense of churning through routine work, but in the sense that a large number of feature areas crossed the finish line in parallel, and the only way to understand what happened is to look at what the completions share structurally rather than naming them individually.

The biggest single track was the physical-resources feature set, which consumed more than two dozen work units and is worth unpacking on its own terms. The core problem this work solves is that Calendar bindings and configuration publications are two distinct authorization surfaces that had been vaguely lumped together under "resource management." Separating them properly meant building out tenant-local Calendar binding authorization, immutable receipt storage for binding identity, and the tenant-denial recovery proof — in that sequence — before touching configuration publication at all. Configuration publication got its own model layer (immutable versions and lineages), a publication intent validator, authorization gating, retry fencing, and a lineage prover. The reason the retry fence matters here is subtle: without it, a failed publication that retries could generate duplicate lineage entries that look like distinct publication events. Getting the fence in before the lineage model is wired to any real persistence means I don't have to audit for that class of corruption later. The final cluster in this track — registration reads, operator caller inputs, the native operator configuration flow, and the repeatable-read snapshot interleaving reproduction — establishes that the reading side of the physical resource model doesn't silently contradict the write side. The snapshot interleaving scenario in particular is a correctness proof that I'd rather have as an explicit test artifact than as an assumption.

Fourteen thin vertical slices across the agent-surface, inbound-channel, agent-eval, and collision surface families closed out today, each following the same eight-stage discipline: contract scope, API hardening, backend orchestration, data determinism, frontend integration, observability, quality gates, and accessibility. That's not busywork — it's the enforcement mechanism that keeps each slice from becoming a placeholder that future work has to interpret. The agent-eval cluster (TV707 through TV711) is the one worth calling out specifically. TV707 established synthetic artifact admission with scoped validation proof retained; TV708 implemented the held-out consequence replay and scoped it to admitted runs only, with replay identity regression recorded; TV709 ran the negative controls and cleanup proof, with calibrated controls and cleanup-verified outcomes published; TV710 added the bounded independence verdict review with assay review kept deliberately separate from scenario status; TV711 captured the recorded ecology snapshot, building immutable partial snapshots and preserving pending recovery separately. The architecture decision running through all five is that independence verdicts should never be derivable from scenario execution status — they're different claims about different things, and conflating them would make the eval loop untrustworthy as an audit surface.

The collision surface family (TV681, TV682, TV683) had a more complicated story than the clean eight-stage pattern suggests. All three slices had seven work units each that had been cancelled at some earlier point — those got audited and restored to planning, which is a signal worth noting: scope that was cancelled prematurely sometimes gets cancelled for reasons that don't survive contact with the actual implementation. The integration closure for each of these (tenant insights, workflow recommendations, operational risk alerts) also needed existing-mount evidence refreshed, which means the mounts were already in place but the integration proof against them had drifted. That's a pattern I want to watch — it shows up when integration tests are written against a snapshot of the API that doesn't match the current shape. The authority-checks-during-rollback retention for TV681 and TV682 was non-obvious: rollback paths have a habit of bypassing the authorization layer because they're treated as administrative operations rather than tenant-scoped ones. Locking that down explicitly now is the kind of thing that looks like paranoia until it isn't.

The Slack evidence producer prerequisite (TV685) closed out its four-stage sequence — trusted workspace admission, atomic normalized evidence, immutable bounded retention, and signed ingress proof with owner handoff — and the main TV685 consumer work was reconciled against the merged producer shape. Same pattern on the SMS side: TV686's consumer was reconciled against the merged SMS producer, and the merged-channel prerequisite provenance was preserved explicitly. The reason this provenance preservation step exists is that the consumer and producer were developed on separate tracks, and without an explicit record of which producer state the consumer was hardened against, future API changes to the producer have no reliable way to determine whether the consumer is still valid. The portfolio-level work archived the owner-merged TV677–TV686 portfolio and reopened the master tracker for 21 audited collision work units — administrative but necessary, because an accurate tracker is the only reliable way to know what's actually done versus what's assumed done.

The open question heading into tomorrow is whether the recorded ecology snapshot approach in TV711 — immutable partial snapshots with pending recovery preserved separately — is the right model at scale, or whether the partiality assumption becomes a liability when recovery isn't prompt. It works cleanly in the current architecture, but the boundary between a pending recovery and a lost snapshot is a policy decision I haven't formalized yet.
