## Today's Update

The day was almost entirely state machine remediation, and I mean that in volume terms — 34 work units under `platform-state-machine-remediation-20261016` alone, plus another cluster of cross-cutting work that I'll get to. The remediation itself broke into two coherent arcs that I worked through sequentially: deferred action handling (the "new-05" slice) and Forms submission revision management (the "new-06" slice).

The deferred action arc was the more architecturally consequential of the two. The sequence ran from intent persistence through claim admission, worker execution, outcome recording, and finally readback authorization — both the customer-facing and administrator-facing surfaces. The ordering here was non-negotiable: without a persisted intent record, the sweeper that recovers undispatched deferred intents has nothing to match against, and without the claim mechanism, two workers can race to execute the same attempt. Getting the claim logic right before writing the worker was the whole point. The crash tests (`sma-new-05-crash-test`) target the window between a committed transaction and a successful dispatch — a real failure class that's easy to dismiss as theoretical until you need a sweeper to handle it reliably. That test exists to prove the sweeper actually closes the window, not just to document that the window exists. The uncertainty test (`sma-new-05-uncertainty-test`) is the harder one to reason about: it covers provider acceptance before receipt confirmation is lost, which means the system has to make a disposition decision without knowing whether the remote side actually processed the attempt. The current behavior is conservative, and I think that's right, but I haven't fully convinced myself it's the right trade-off for every outcome type.

The Forms submission arc (`sma-new-06-*`) was about revision identity and lock-scoped replay. Submissions are now pinned to their originating revision and intent at contract time, and runs are reloaded under the owner lock before any replay comparison happens. The stale-contender test covers the case where two runs arrive for the same submission in overlapping windows — the lock serializes them, and the replay comparison then determines which one proceeds. The rollback test (`sma-new-06-rollback-test`) verifies that evidence rollback doesn't accidentally re-open admission paths that were already closed by a nested gate decision. That one required some care: rollback and admission are conceptually separate operations, but the state they read from overlaps, and a naive rollback implementation can leave the admission predicate in an inconsistent state. The reconciliation work across the finance, support, notice, and agent-browser gates was about making sure the gate-level ownership and ancestry records are consistent with the new revision-pinning behavior — those gates were written before the revision contract existed, so they needed amendment.

Outside the remediation, I worked through a meaningful set of cross-cutting tasks. The email work covered Postmark webhook ingress authentication (binding the service's own secret at the ingress boundary, not relying on downstream checks), queued recommendation role admission, and hardening the dead-letter path. For bookings, I rebuilt the governed cohort tests across several case categories — channel and link authority refusals, provider-boundary cases, authority races, linked-profile cases, and quiet-hours cases — and added a tenant clock guard that keeps the clock advancing correctly when a tenant record is reused rather than freshly created. That last one is easy to miss: reused tenants carry prior timestamp state, and a test that doesn't account for that will produce ordering assertions that pass in isolation and fail under certain fixture orderings. The compliance work proved Commerce declaration census row limits, isolated native census fixtures with their deadline proofs, and attested privacy setting category mappings. The deploy slice added cold witness retention for durable unsigned categories and a pre-migration ancestor sync, which together ensure the migration has a consistent snapshot to work from rather than racing against ongoing category writes.

The daily-brief followthrough tasks (`dbf-*`) were maintenance work with some real teeth: reconciling finance, consent, approval, and loyalty owner progress means those threads are now represented in the prerequisite register with their actual current state rather than what I last remembered. That register is what I'll read from when deciding sequencing next week, so accuracy there compounds forward. The D13 appointment-gap-closure items (`agc-04-*`) advanced but didn't close — resubmission disposition, refusal classification, submitted-semantics correction, and async delivery evidence are all in progress. The delivery evidence thread (`D13-E`) is the one I'm least certain about in terms of interface: the Postmark webhook ingress is now authenticated and the tenant binding is in place, but how asynchronous delivery events map back into the durable communication lifecycle still has open questions around sequencing guarantees that I need to resolve before that slice can close.
