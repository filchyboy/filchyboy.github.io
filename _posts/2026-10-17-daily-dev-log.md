## Today's Update

The day had two centers of gravity that are connected in a way that only became fully clear by the end: the state machine remediation work, which touched nearly every gate and transition contract in the platform, and the email/D13 arc, which is where async delivery evidence is being built into the durable communication lifecycle for the first time. They're connected because both are ultimately about the same problem — what happens to a record when the system can't confirm that the other side received something.

The state machine remediation (`platform-state-machine-remediation-20261016`) produced the most volume today, 34 work units across gate reconciliation, deferred action handling, and Forms run integrity. The gate reconciliation items — finance cutover ancestry, D13 and delivery owner alignment, merged agent and open browser work, ST-01/ST-08 ownership — are the kind of thing where the code looks fine in isolation but accumulates silent inconsistency when you trace what each gate actually accepts at transition time. The finance and replay acceptance amendments specifically narrow what evidence is treated as valid: the finance gate had criteria broad enough to accept state that shouldn't have cleared, and the replay acceptance was similarly loose. Neither would explode in an obvious way; they'd just allow subtly wrong transitions to be recorded as legitimate. The Forms submission work built on top of that: pinning revision and intent to the contract, persisting the command identity, reloading runs under owner locks, and comparing replay payloads under those same locks. The lock-before-compare ordering is deliberate — comparing a stale read under concurrent load is worse than not comparing at all, because it produces false confidence rather than an honest failure. The recovery and stale-contender tests (`sma-new-06-stale-test`, `sma-new-06-replay-test`, `sma-new-06-rollback-test`) close the loop on whether the locking and replay logic actually holds under the adversarial cases I care about: two concurrent owners, a changed input, and an evidence rollback with nested admission.

The deferred action work (`sma-new-05-*`) is where I spent the most concentrated effort. Claim, dispatch, worker, sweeper, outcome, readback, UI, accessibility — this is a full vertical slice of durable action execution, and the crash test (`sma-new-05-crash-test`) is the unit I kept coming back to mentally while writing the rest. "Death after commit before dispatch" is exactly the failure the sweeper exists to handle: the intent is persisted, the worker dies before the job enters the queue, and the sweeper recovers undispatched intents on the next pass. Whether the sweeper's detection window is tight enough is something I'll need to verify empirically — the logic is correct at the unit level, but the timing assumptions baked into the recovery interval are not something a test can fully validate. The response type separation (submission outcomes vs. action outcomes) mattered more than it might appear: mixing those two into a single type would have obscured which part of the pipeline produced a given result, making observability and future debugging harder. Separating them now while the surface is still small is the right call even though it added work.

The email work picked up the D13-E thread — asynchronous delivery evidence. Authenticating the Postmark webhook ingress with the service's own secret was the first step (S1), and everything else in the email cluster flows from that: binding the service's tenant in the Postmark processor, qualifying correlation on MySQL and through the governed path, splitting attempt matching out of the correlation task, and hardening the credential and dead-letter admission path. The correlation work is the piece I'm least certain about in terms of long-term shape. Right now the MySQL qualification and governed path qualification are proven separately; whether they need to be unified under a single correlation contract or can stay parallel is a design question I haven't resolved. The D13-B, C, D, E items in appointment-gap-closure are all marked in-progress rather than complete — meaning today moved them forward but didn't close them. That's accurate. D13-E in particular depends on the full delivery evidence pipeline being exercisable end-to-end, which requires more of the async path to be wired before I can declare the lifecycle closed. The remaining work in bookings — exact committed reschedule replay, exact approved cancellation replay, the governed cohort expansions for channel authority, provider boundaries, authority races, and quiet-hours cases — built out the test surface for cases that are easy to reason about individually but interact in ways that are hard to predict without explicit coverage. Requiring exact replay on both reschedule and cancellation is a stronger assertion than it might look: it rules out any replay path that reconstructs a plausible-looking but non-identical payload, which is the kind of silent divergence that would corrupt audit trails without causing immediate test failures.

Tomorrow the D13 arc needs to advance. D13-B through D13-E are all in-progress, and the async delivery evidence path specifically needs the Postmark correlation and the durable lifecycle integration to meet somewhere concrete. The state machine work is in better shape — the gate criteria, lock discipline, and deferred action infrastructure are all tested — but I want to review the sweeper's recovery interval assumptions before treating that slice as closed.
