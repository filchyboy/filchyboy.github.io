## Today's Update

Today was dense across an unusual number of fronts simultaneously — privacy, compliance, bookings, staging infrastructure, and the admin shell unification effort all moved in parallel. The common thread underneath most of it was getting data custody boundaries right before those paths become anything a future operator would exercise at scale.

The largest single block of work landed in the privacy and compliance modules, and the two are tightly coupled enough that separating them in retrospect feels artificial. The privacy work centered on DSR (data subject request) handling: binding signed source facts to exact custody records, scoping metadata review authority, preserving DSR scope in direct data readers, and installing freshly-verified DSR source publications. What I was solving here isn't just "store the right data" — it's making sure that when a DSR arrives, the system can prove, from immutable evidence, what was captured and under what authority at every stage. That requires the custody bindings to be exact and the verification to happen outside database locks (which is where the compliance work crossed in). I added live source verification outside lock boundaries specifically because holding a DB lock through a cryptographic verification step creates a contention surface that would be painful to fix later. The compliance module also got a typed live Core completion boundary exposed, which lets downstream consumers make decisions based on a verified type rather than an assumed one — a small surface area change with meaningful downstream consequences. Whether I got the exact scope of the retention deferral right (`compliance-defer-retention-without-exact-source`) is something I'll need to verify when the full DSR pipeline runs end-to-end. The logic is correct in isolation, but the boundary where "exact source authority" is determined touches the custody binding work, and I want an integration sequence before I call that solid.

The bookings work covered a lot of lifecycle ground. Customer self-service reschedule landed with email-verified grants as the admission mechanism — the constraint being that a reschedule shouldn't succeed unless the identity chain is intact at the time of the request, not just at the time of booking. Customer cancellation is now bound to a reviewed refund consequence, which means the cancel path can't silently absorb a payment without first establishing what the refund disposition should be. The contact disclosure model also got real structure: contact is withheld until privacy lifecycle admission, the owner-only contact view in the bookings list is behind a disclosure audit, and customers can withdraw their disclosure with revocation proofs retained. That last piece — the revocation proof — is the kind of thing that looks like ceremony until a regulatory question arrives. I also added the `/appointments/manage` customer reschedule page and corrected the owner bookings page path, both of which are navigational necessities before the booking journey can run end-to-end without dead routes. Owner-approved organizer cancellation with full unwind also landed, which required getting the unwind sequence right — every resource reservation and financial commitment needs to be released in the correct order, and the approval gate exists to prevent a unilateral cancellation from triggering a partial unwind.

Staging got a cluster of hardening work that's primarily about making the environment trustworthy before I start using it to validate anything meaningful. I isolated the application behind a fixed loopback ingress (so nothing reaches the app through an uncontrolled surface), hardened Python package permissions before validation runs, isolated Colima observer credentials in a private volume, and separated the application and bootstrap environments. The credential isolation is the one I felt most strongly about — Colima observer access shouldn't be visible to the application environment, full stop. I also published admin assets using the pinned image shell and recorded archive validation version history, which gives me a traceable record when future validation runs diverge from expected baselines.

The admin shell unification work progressed across 19 routes today — identity jurisdictions, broker inspection, infrastructure health, the catalog subtree, agent egress, MCP analytics, and others. None of these are complete yet (they're all marked `in_progress`), but the pattern is consistent: each route is getting its content island integrated into the unified shell rather than living as an isolated legacy blade. The logging route (`asu-route-admin-logging`) is being qualified as retained Laravel content rather than migrated, which is the right call for surfaces where the server-rendered view genuinely serves the use case. The admin shell work is the least glamorous part of the day but it matters structurally — a fragmented admin experience creates operational confusion when future operators need to diagnose something across multiple surface areas quickly.
