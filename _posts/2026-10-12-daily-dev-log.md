## Today's Update

Thirty-eight units across eleven feature areas — the breadth is real, but there are a few through-lines worth naming rather than treating this as a scatter-shot day.

The heaviest sequence was the CVG repository readiness work, which I'd been staging for a while and finally pushed through in one sustained pass. Sixteen units in total, and the ordering mattered: `cvgr-admission` established what portfolio admission and owner overlap actually mean before anything downstream could reference it, then the ownership and concurrency contracts (`cvgr-owner-contract`) gave the evidence layer a stable identity to reason about. From there the sequence moved through completion receipts, review evidence resolution (`cvgr-review-evidence` against issue #6107), and revalidation at decision time — that last one being the constraint that prevents stale evidence from passing through a review gate that's since moved. The cutover fence (`cvgr-cutover-fence`) protects current evidence through the transition window, which is the kind of guard that's invisible when things go right and catastrophic when it's missing. The recovery side of this — `cvgr-recovery-intent`, `cvgr-recovery-worker` — had to come after the ownership contracts were settled, because financial recovery needs to know what "original reservation identity" means before it can reconcile committed spend against bounded claims. I also inventoried all legacy worker queues and producers (`cvgr-queue-inventory`) and specified the compatibility rollout receipt, which is groundwork I'll be grateful for when the queue migration becomes a real decision point. Whether the rollout receipt format I landed on is forward-compatible enough, I'm genuinely not certain — queue topology changes have a way of invalidating assumptions baked into receipts, and I'd want a second pass on that before treating it as stable. Closing out the CVG work: `cvgr-review-proof` establishes the test posture that forged and stale review inputs get refused at the boundary, and `cvgr-refresh-proof` proves readback and recovery accessibility. The separation of draft edits from authoritative refresh (`cvgr-refresh`) is the design decision I feel most confident about in this whole batch — conflating those two paths is how data integrity bugs hide.

The appointments work was a coherent arc of its own. Owner-chosen booking windows, cadence, buffers, and explicit cancellation cutoff landed as a single unit, which is the right granularity — those four configuration knobs are interdependent enough that separating them into sequential releases would have required holding partial state in the UI between deploys. The intake form selection and optional Google mirror in booking-link setup followed, then the publication review gate: requiring explicit human approval before a booking link goes live, with publication review bound to the effective intake profile. That binding is load-bearing. If the intake profile that was reviewed isn't the one that's active at publication time, the approval becomes meaningless. Getting that assertion into the domain model rather than relying on process discipline is the right call for a path that future operators will exercise without necessarily understanding the review intent. PHPMD findings in the booking-link setup files also got cleared — not glamorous, but static analysis debt in setup flows compounds quickly because those files tend to accumulate conditions.

Privacy category management and compliance work closed out the day's more defensive posture. Canonicalizing the category semantic and presentation digests (`col-6038-category-canonical-digest`) alongside the reviewed determination digest means the comparison surface is now deterministic — two records that should match will hash consistently regardless of insertion order or field serialization quirks. That kind of canonicalization is one of those things that seems like over-engineering until you're debugging a divergence that only appears in certain locale configurations. On the compliance side: bounding catalog export validation complexity, preserving distinct impact and approval actors, and rejecting both malformed approval receipt shapes and scalar catalog export payloads. The scalar rejection is worth a brief note — a payload that arrives as a scalar instead of a structured type would previously have produced an ambiguous validation failure; now it's an explicit refusal with a defined shape. Future code touching this path will know exactly what the input contract requires.

Intelligence and tracker work was lighter but deliberate. Distinguishing agent optimization from execution authority (`intelligence-distinguish-agent-optimization-from-execution`) is a conceptual boundary that I want formalized early, before any agent surface is exercised under real load. An agent that can optimize a plan and one that can execute against live resources are not the same thing, and the places where that distinction blurs are where the hard authorization questions live. The tracker additions for external principal authentication and cross-plan features give the roadmap surface more structure. The bookings work — verifying protected hold reservation conflicts and normalizing delayed empty intake profiles with Google blocker explanations — rounds out the day.

Tomorrow's immediate priority is deciding whether the queue compatibility receipt format from the CVG work needs a revision pass before the legacy queue inventory becomes an active migration input. That's the one thing from today I'd flag as potentially needing revisiting sooner rather than later.
