## Today's Update

Thirty-eight units across eleven feature areas — the breadth is real, but the day had a center of gravity: the CVG repository readiness pass. That cluster absorbed the most deliberate sequencing, and it's worth explaining why the order mattered.

The CVG work started with admission and ownership — refreshing portfolio admission, establishing evidence ownership contracts, and defining what concurrency between owner operations looks like. Those contracts had to precede everything downstream. Once ownership was unambiguous, I could build the operational completion receipt for #6109 and then resolve source-backed review evidence for #6107, which is the kind of sequencing that looks obvious in retrospect but requires the earlier contract to be explicit. From there: revalidating review evidence at decision time, binding verified evidence before transfer-ready state, protecting current evidence through cutover effects. The cutover fence in particular is the kind of guard that only matters when something goes wrong at a specific phase boundary — but it's also exactly the thing that's cheapest to define during initial construction and most expensive to retrofit. I followed that with the financial recovery sequence: persisting recovery intent before orphaned effects can accumulate, then reconciling committed spend with bounded claims in the recovery worker. The queue inventory and compatibility rollout receipt closed out the operational side — I catalogued all legacy worker queues and producers, then specified how compatibility rolls out without breaking existing consumers. The final two items were proof artifacts: refusing forged and stale review input, proving readback and accessible recovery. Having those proofs written as distinct artifacts means the invariants aren't just asserted in comments — they're exercised. Whether the proof coverage is complete enough for the eventual production path, I'm genuinely uncertain; the review input refusal test covers the cases I've anticipated, but the space of stale-input scenarios at cutover time is wider than any static test suite can fully enumerate.

The appointments work was the other meaningful sequence today. Owner-chosen booking window, cadence, buffers, and explicit cancellation cutoff all landed as a unit, which is the right shape for that configuration surface — these parameters interact, and defining them independently invites inconsistency. The intake form selection and optional Google Calendar mirror followed, then publication review requiring explicit human approval before a booking link goes live. That approval gate is a deliberate friction point. The alternative — letting the system auto-publish once configuration passes validation — would be faster, but it also means a misconfigured link could be distributed before anyone has actually looked at it. Binding publication review to the effective intake profile closes that gap: approval and the thing being approved are evaluated at the same moment, not at separate phases. I also cleared PHPMD findings in the booking-link setup files while I was working through that area — not because static analysis findings always indicate real problems, but because carrying known findings makes it harder to notice new ones.

Privacy category management got two canonicalization passes — semantic and presentation digests for categories, and the reviewed determination digest. These matter because the digest values are what downstream comparisons key on; if the canonicalization logic is inconsistent, comparisons produce false negatives that are genuinely hard to debug. Grouping category semantics and simplifying validation in the main privacy module was a companion move. The compliance work followed a consistent pattern: bounding catalog export validation complexity, preserving distinct impact and approval actors, and rejecting both malformed approval receipt shapes and scalar catalog export payloads. That last one — rejecting scalar payloads — exists because the export shape is a contract boundary where a type downgrade would silently pass schema validation but break downstream processing. The bookings side handled protected hold reservation conflicts and normalized delayed empty intake profiles, with an explanation of Google Calendar blockers baked into the latter. The intelligence feature set got its October 12 briefing and delivery receipt reconciled, plus a boundary definition separating agent optimization authority from execution authority — a distinction that will matter more as the agent surface grows.

The track running underneath most of this is making decision boundaries explicit before they're exercised. The approval gate before booking link publication, the cutover fence protecting review evidence, the scalar payload rejection — none of these are defensive reactions to observed failures. They're assertions about what the system should refuse, written while the construction is still open. That's cheaper now than after the first future operator runs into an edge the system didn't anticipate.
