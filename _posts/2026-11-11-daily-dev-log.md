## Today's Update

The day spread across more domains than usual — thirteen feature areas with meaningful depth in most of them, which made the sequencing choices consequential. Rather than moving through one area start to finish, the work threaded between three structural layers: activating product surfaces, hardening the admission ownership model, and closing out a set of marketing and compliance slices that had been accumulating loose ends.

The admission work was the heaviest lift. Two parallel tracks ran simultaneously: the `admission-inventory-allocation` migration sequence and the broader `admission` waitlist ownership model. The migration track covered the full lifecycle transition — backfilling active booking allocations, migrating approval and reschedule transitions, implementing recoverable release, and retiring the legacy capacity check paths. Each of those involved fencing the old write paths before cutting reads over to the new owner state, so the order was strict: writer fences first, then read cutover, then the legacy confirmation retirement. The waitlist ownership track was structurally separate but conceptually adjacent — issuing bounded offers from current consent, handling expiry atomically with a scheduled sweep, proving that claim and expiry serialize correctly under contention, and enforcing Event authority at the payment claim boundary. The serialization proof (`admission-prove-waitlist-claim-expiry-serialize`) was the piece I spent the most time on; getting that test to exercise the actual race rather than a simulation of it required care in how the transaction boundaries were arranged. I'm reasonably confident the coverage is correct, but it's the kind of test where I'll want a second pass before this path sees any exercise.

The venue physical identity work filled out a complete foundation: schema and model for tenant-owned physical identity, a resolver, Calendar binding persistence with a uniqueness constraint, configuration version schema and model, policy authorization for both binding and configuration publication, the mutation actions, and the query surface. The important invariant throughout was tenant isolation — the `venue-physical-tenant-test` and `venue-calendar-binding-test` proofs verify that neither a physical identity nor its Calendar binding can be read or written across tenant boundaries, even when the underlying schema would technically permit it. The configuration retry fencing test (`venue-config-retry-test`) covers the idempotency side: a configuration publication that retries after a partial write should produce the same committed state, not a duplicate version. The venue contract review happened last and confirmed the P03 surface is consistent with what the schema and model express — no gaps I need to revisit immediately.

The product surface and thin-slice work ran in parallel with the above. TV725 (Product Surface Effective Activation) and TV724 (Product Surface Domain Overlay) both completed their full sequences — contract baseline, approval and separation-of-duties enforcement, backend orchestration, data determinism, API contract hardening, frontend integration, observability, and focused test/accessibility review. TV724's domain overlay logic sits underneath that — it's what gives a scoped release its domain context when the activation fires. The two slices needed to be completed in that dependency order. Marketing added two more complete slices: TV746 (Lifecycle Trigger Follow-Up) and TV744 (Campaign Pause/Cancel), both through quality gates and handoff. The campaign pause/cancel work in particular connects to the email-layer campaign control recovery items, which I also closed today — fencing resume replay, preserving control recovery state, and recording the TV744 review fixes. Those email items are the runtime expression of what the marketing slice defines contractually.
