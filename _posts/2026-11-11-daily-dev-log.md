## Today's Update

Two hundred and three completed units across thirty-three feature areas is a large number, but the number isn't the point. What today actually was: a wide-front consolidation pass that closed off whole domains rather than advancing individual slices. The admission inventory system, the venue physical identity layer, the product surface activation and domain overlay loops, the marketing campaign control sequences — all of these reached a state where the work transitions from "building the mechanism" to "relying on it." That transition is what I was actually aiming for.

The admission work was the most structurally involved. The inventory allocation feature set now has the full arc from hold creation through release, covering cancellation, rejection, no-show, reschedule, and waitlist offer expiry. Getting there required explicit writer fences — `admission-ga-create-writer-fence`, `admission-ga-appointment-writer-fence`, `admission-ga-notification-writer-fence`, and several more — that prevent legacy paths from producing competing writes once the owner-based model is authoritative. I also backfilled active booking allocations and durable waitlist offers through migration tasks, reconciled notified-but-no-offer entries, and retired the duplicate booking confirmation path. The parallel `admission` feature set went even deeper: bounded waitlist offer issuance, contention and recovery proofs, claim serialization against expiry, checkout hold operation upgrades, and payment claim authority enforcement. The specific thing I want to flag is the design choice around expiry: `admission-expire-identified-waitlist-offers-atomically` does exactly what it says — single atomic write, no intermediate states — which matters when the expiry sweep and a simultaneous claim attempt arrive at the same record. I proved that serialization holds in `admission-prove-waitlist-claim-expiry-serialize`. Whether that proof is comprehensive enough for all MySQL isolation modes is something I still want a second look at before this path is exercised at any real volume.

The venue physical identity layer is a complete foundation: schema and model for the tenant-owned physical record, Calendar binding persistence with uniqueness enforcement, configuration version migration and model, the binding and configuration action classes, and the resolver implementation — all the way through authorization policies and the read query surface. I ended that sequence with tenant isolation, Calendar binding uniqueness, and configuration retry fencing tests. That's the right order: define the structure, implement the mutations, then prove the invariants. The retry fencing test (`venue-config-retry-test`) is the one that matters most for operator safety — if a configuration version write can be replayed and produce a second record, the version lineage becomes unreliable. It can't, and now there's evidence that it can't.

For the thin vertical slices, TV725 (Product Surface Effective Activation) is fully closed — all nine phases from contract scope through accessibility evidence. TV724 (Product Surface Domain Overlay) is closed at seven phases. The marketing slices TV746 (Lifecycle Trigger Follow-Up) and TV744 (Campaign Pause Cancel) are also complete with quality gates and handoff documented. TV700 and TV706 account for another thirty-one units between them, covering successor activation lifecycle controls and the full tenant application rollback mechanism — atomicity proofs, contention qualification, governed rollback telemetry, and the workbench mounting for both tenant-owned and governed paths. The TV706 governed rollback work is the one I'm least confident about from a completeness standpoint: distinguishing a competing rollback from a replay (`tv706-distinguish-competing-governed-rollback-from-replay`) is a correctness boundary that's easy to describe but harder to prove exhaustively. The current test is scoped to MySQL behavior I've observed; it may need extension when the governed authority surface expands.

The remaining breadth — privacy category evidence, compliance scheme authority, admin Customer Operations island, settings draft preservation, analytics export lifecycle, frontend bundle budget, gamification session revocation — represents the platform's outer layer getting tighter contracts and cleaner invariants before any of those surfaces are exercised in an integrated way. The settings work around dotted key validation and keeping encrypted values out of admin responses is the kind of thing that's easy to retrofit incorrectly, so getting it right at contract time is worth the care. Same logic applies to the compliance work: `compliance-enforce-current-scheme-publisher-authority` and `compliance-require-exact-successor-publication-receipts` define the authority model precisely, which means the UI layer never has to invent its own interpretation of what's permitted.
