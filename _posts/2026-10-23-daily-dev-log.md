The email contacts CDP cutover metadata and markdownlint item is the one I'd flag specifically: metadata that doesn't match the actual cutover state is the kind of thing that causes a future operator to make a wrong assumption about whether a migration has been completed. Getting that aligned with a lint pass in the same unit keeps the fix verifiable rather than just editorial. The DHA closeout health work is still in progress — there are remaining blockers I didn't fully resolve today, and I'd rather leave that accurately tracked than close it prematurely.

The compliance cluster was the largest single body of technical work and it all orbits the same problem: scheme-wide owner coverage needs to be provable, not assumed. I added a scheme-wide owner census contract, then built the enforcement logic around it — snapshots fail closed when the roster can't be sealed, local scheme impact is rechecked under mutation lock, and the capture resolver wiring was kept inside the complexity guard deliberately. That last constraint matters more than it might look: resolver wiring that grows unbounded in complexity is how admission logic becomes untestable. The proposal review surface now exposes admitted scheme publication receipts, and there's a test proving the keyboard read path works. Seven consent items ran in parallel — bounding and validating the scheme census source scan, proving the census against a disposable MySQL instance, rejecting post-cutoff arrivals, and verifying the nonempty range lock and isolation guard. The MySQL disposition custody fixture got refreshed as part of that pass. Running the census proof against a disposable database rather than a shared fixture is a deliberate choice: it means the test owns its entire data surface and can't silently pass because of leftover state from a previous run.

The workspace changes dealt with admission policy enforcement across several read and write paths. Approval owner admission is now rechecked before projecting evidence, source models pass through the admission gate, and intervention evidence owners are revalidated on recovery reads. The trust policy evidence retirement is worth calling out separately — removing unsupported evidence from the policy evaluation path is the kind of change that's easy to defer and hard to clean up later once call sites accumulate. The attention limits are applied after source admission, which preserves the correct evaluation order: admit first, then limit, rather than limiting before you know what you're working with. The workspace items and the compliance cluster share an underlying pattern I keep returning to: the boundary between "what the caller asserts" and "what the system independently verifies" needs to be explicit in the code, not implicit in convention.

The evidence-driven productization work resolved three admission questions that had been open: what the authoritative billing tax context actually is, whether CRM orchestration is supportable in the current architecture (it isn't, and that's now explicit), and which Slack operations are actually implemented versus aspirational. Rejecting unsupported CRM orchestration as a documented decision is more useful than leaving it as an unmarked gap — a future implementer reading the planning record will know this was considered and declined rather than overlooked. On the surface-ownership side, I extracted a shared production-route render helper and added host-shell journey coverage for the Service Hub routes, both of which reduce the per-route setup cost for anything added to that surface later. The PHPStan harness evidence and backlog links were also restored, which closes a gap in the audit trail for the static analysis work.

The open thread going into tomorrow is the DHA closeout health item. There are specific blockers I haven't resolved, and I want to understand whether they're documentation gaps or whether they're pointing at something in the underlying planning record that needs correction. That distinction changes what the fix looks like.
