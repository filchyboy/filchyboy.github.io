## Today's Update

Today was a convergence day — not in the sense that everything came together cleanly, but in the sense that a half-dozen parallel threads had to reach a common finish line before September 23rd becomes a real constraint rather than an aspirational date. The Revyrie demo readiness work has been running in the background across multiple audit rounds, and today I closed it out properly. That meant three distinct passes: the Round 1 audit established canonical state, pinned scheduler and Checkout entry regressions, proved the Price Change boundary suite twice (the second pass matters — a test that passes once is evidence, a test that passes consistently is a gate), and published the delta. Round 2 then rescanned credible candidate wedges, smoke-tested them locally, adapted deterministic fixtures for both a primary and a fallback tenant, implemented the scoped campaign reset, and proved fixture idempotency and tenant isolation in separate passes. Round 3 closed by dispositioning every remaining P1, finalizing both demo scripts, aligning claims and diligence answers, and running focused quality gates for each wedge. The final readiness audit on top of that — five steps from state establishment through diligence package production — was the formal close-out. I'm not going to overstate what this produces: it's a reproducible demo environment with a documented fallback path, not a production-hardened deployment. But that distinction matters, and the audit package now says so explicitly.

The two thin vertical slices — persistent cart resume/abandonment (TV-867) and shipping address validation with rate selection (TV-868) — each went end-to-end today. Contract scope baseline, backend orchestration, data determinism, API contract hardening, frontend integration, observability instrumentation, quality gates, focused tests, and accessibility for both. The pattern is mechanical once the contract is defined, but the contract work is where the real decisions live. For cart recovery specifically, the lifecycle transitions needed explicit hardening in `commerce-checkout` — recovery can't silently overwrite a confirmed payment reference, and mismatched captures need to be retained rather than discarded. The checkout layer now has both behaviors covered: `retain-mismatched-payment-references` and `keep-mismatched-captures-from-overwriting-confirmed-payments` are distinct paths, not consolidated into a single guard, because the failure modes are different and the audit trail should reflect that.

The `cvg-001` (capability inventory) and `cvg-003` (native capability fit) feature sets both completed their full implementation sequences today — 22 units each, covering everything from baseline refresh through completion archive. `cvg-005` (capability value evidence) was larger at 30 units and included an onboarding sub-sequence that deserves a note: the "Understand the work" panel isn't just a UI affordance. The observation revision and authority proofs (`cvg005-onb-06`, `cvg005-onb-07`, `cvg005-onb-08`) exist because the distinction between a workflow observation and evidence derived from that observation has to be enforced at the data boundary, not just communicated in the UI. If those two concepts blur, owner correction workflows become unreliable. I also finished the native capability fit assessment work in the provider abstraction layer, which is what `cvg-003` feeds into downstream — the transfer draft handoff DTO (`cvg003-11`) is the bridge between fit assessment and the actual provider transition flow.

The conformance layer absorbed a significant batch of work around CORS bridge behavior — 24 units covering preflight header handling, bridged body contract strictness, browser-optional assertions, and deterministic coverage of exchange sequences. The significant decisions here were around optionality versus strictness: `allow-browser-origin-optional` and `keep-bridged-body-contract-strict` are in deliberate tension, and the resolution is that the browser origin can be absent in non-browser contexts, but when a body is present it must be validated strictly. The budget derivation work — `derive-expected-request-budget-from-flow` and `derive-budget-from-recorded-exchanges` — matters because hard-coded exchange totals are a maintenance trap. Once you have more than a handful of conformance scenarios, someone will update the flow and forget to update the constant. The integration governance work (binding external event graphs, covering conflict paths, encoding binding invariants, rejecting numeric-string revisions) runs adjacent to this and is similarly defensive: the invariant encoding exists so that a future integration surface can't silently accept a revision that would have been rejected at the schema layer.

The authentication fixes for the demo environment are worth being direct about. The Revyrie audit surfaced that demo-tenant actors couldn't authenticate cleanly — `admit-unique-demo-tenant-web-login-without-tenant-scope-leakage` and `ignore-posted-system-tenant-discriminators-on-web-login` were direct responses to P0-1 and P0-2 from the Round 2 audit. The failed-login lookup scope fix (`scope-failed-login-lookup-by-tenant-discriminator`) is the one I'm least certain about in terms of long-term correctness — it's the right behavior for the demo environment, but I'll want to revalidate that the scoping doesn't create a lookup blindspot when the same email appears across tenants in a multi-tenant production context. That's a note-to-self for when the platform is exercised with real tenant data, not today's problem.
