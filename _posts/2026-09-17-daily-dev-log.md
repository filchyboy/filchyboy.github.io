## Today's Update

September 17 was a convergence day — multiple long-running threads reaching their natural close points simultaneously. The Revyrie demo readiness campaign finished its three-round arc, two checkout thin vertical slices shipped end-to-end, three capability governance feature sets crossed the archive line, and a dense cluster of conformance infrastructure got settled. That's an unusual amount of closure for a single day, and it's worth being precise about what actually closed versus what just moved.

The Revyrie demo readiness work was the most operationally consequential thing I finished. Round 1 addressed the three hard blockers: scheduler command admission was broken and got repaired with a regression pin, checkout entry resolution was wired incorrectly and got corrected with a proof of both development and production loading, and the Price Change test suite had two independent failures — one in the tenant harness and one in the telemetry expectation — both corrected and proved twice. Round 2 then addressed a different category of problem: the authentication side. Demo-tenant actors couldn't authenticate, pending migrations were leaving the readiness endpoint at 503, and fixture credentials had no documented provision path. I worked through the P0 blockers in sequence and documented the P1 risks — `APP_URL` misalignment, Fluent Bit, MFA grace period, route prefix drift — with explicit dispositions rather than leaving them as ambient concerns. Round 3 closed the campaign: final primary and fallback wedge selection, deterministic fixture adaptation, scoped campaign reset implementation with idempotency and tenant isolation proofs, and the final summary published. The authentication work that supported this — admitting unique demo-tenant web login without tenant-scope leakage, scoping failed-login lookup by tenant discriminator, ignoring posted system-tenant discriminators on the web login path — was genuinely fiddly. The tenant discriminator scoping in particular required care to avoid a class of lookup that would bleed across tenant boundaries during the authentication flow without any obvious error signal.

The two checkout thin vertical slices — persistent cart resume and abandonment (TV-867) and shipping address validation with rate selection (TV-868) — each went through the full eight-stage sequence: contract scope baseline, backend orchestration, data determinism, API contract hardening, frontend integration, observability instrumentation, quality gates, and focused tests with accessibility coverage. Both are now archived. The persistence side of TV-867 required thinking carefully about what "resume" means when a cart's underlying inventory state may have changed since it was abandoned — the data determinism work is where that gets pinned, not the API layer, and I made sure the contract reflected that boundary explicitly. TV-868's rate selection surface required the frontend integration to handle the case where no valid rates are returned cleanly, rather than silently presenting an empty selection that looks valid.

The three capability governance feature sets — `cvg-001` (capability inventory), `cvg-003` (native capability fit), and `cvg-005` (capability value evidence) — all reached their archive points today. CVG-005 was the most involved of the three: beyond the standard backend/frontend/documentation/archive sequence, it included an onboarding observation sub-track — specifying bounded workflow observations, persisting revision history, reading scoped process evidence, recording operator correction, and rendering the "Understand the work" panel. The evidence disagreement workflow and assessment disposition transition hardening in CVG-005 are worth noting as design decisions: rather than treating evidence conflicts as exceptional states to be handled in error paths, I modeled them as first-class workflow states with their own transition logic. That makes future dispute handling explicit in the domain rather than implicit in the controller. The conformance work ran in parallel across the day — 24 units cleaning up the CORS bridge testing infrastructure. The important architectural decision there was making CORS bridge coverage deterministic by deriving the expected request budget from recorded exchanges rather than hard-coding exchange totals. Hard-coded counts are a maintenance trap; they fail silently when new exchanges are added and they don't communicate intent.

Forward from here: the capability governance trilogy being archived means the stewardship and provider abstraction layers now have three fully-documented interfaces to build against. The demo readiness closure means the September 23 Revyrie demo has a scripted primary path, a scripted fallback, and verified fixture isolation. The remaining open thread I'm watching is how the pricing and commerce work — cross-tenant price-change projection rejection, refund retention guards, refund rejection predicates — composes with the checkout slices that shipped today. Those aren't the same layer, but they touch overlapping state, and I want to confirm the boundary is clean before the next checkout slice starts.
