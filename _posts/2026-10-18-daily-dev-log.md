## Today's Update

Today was dispersed across more surface area than I typically prefer — 19 feature areas touched, which is a signal that I was working through a lot of connective tissue rather than driving any single thread to completion. The two largest concentrations were DSR and bookings, and those are worth unpacking separately because they were doing different kinds of work.

The DSR cluster was the densest thread. Thirteen units, and they weren't cosmetically related — they formed an actual dependency chain. I started by isolating the declaration fixture environment, which needed to happen before any of the binding work could produce trustworthy results. From there: preserving historical declaration source definitions, verifying the published historical source snapshot, binding declaration source and tenant facts to assignment, and excluding unowned declaration category claims. The purpose-category alignment work came next — requiring that purpose data categories equal the owner union, validating purpose category before declaration publication, binding the declaration purpose to a governed category digest. The tail of that chain handled counting bound processing purpose impact references, verifying activation history for purpose impact, making completed governed export replays no-ops, rejecting multiple processing purpose bindings, and retaining source binding cutoff precision. The ordering of this chain was the real work. Each step assumes the previous one has narrowed the state space: you can't meaningfully validate purpose-category alignment before you've established what categories the declaration is even allowed to reference, and you can't make replays idempotent before you've established what "completed" means under governed export semantics. The precision-retention step at the end — carrying microsecond-level cutoff timestamps rather than rounding to seconds — is the kind of thing that seems pedantic until a future audit needs to determine which source binding was active at a specific moment.

The bookings thread ran nine units and had a different character — more about decomposing logic that had accumulated in the wrong place than about building new capability. Moving notice source currency checks into a task and separating guard lookup from validation are both extractions: taking logic that lived inline and giving it a home where it can be tested and evolved independently. The authority probe becoming a parameter object (in the comms feature set, but tightly related) is the same instinct. The concurrency work was more load-bearing: atomic reschedule grant consumption and the assertion that exactly one retirement and one advance occur across concurrent workers are correctness properties that need to hold under any scheduling, not just the happy path. The attention-state preservation across pages and classifying communication refusals against the D13-C/D13-B vocabulary round out the bookings work — the vocabulary binding matters because future operators extending the refusal classification won't have to reverse-engineer which terms were in scope at the time this was designed.

The login-history slice is worth a brief note because of the timestamp precision theme that appeared independently in DSR: preserving fractional review cutoffs and retaining microseconds on export reviews. Two separate feature sets arrived at the same decision for the same reason — when export review evidence is used in an audit or reconciliation context, truncated timestamps create ambiguity about ordering that can't be recovered after the fact. I didn't plan these two to land on the same day; they converged because the underlying requirement is real. The compliance and checkout threads both formalized a pattern I've been working toward for a while: the publication lock (or source lock) needs to be held during the census guard check, not acquired before and released before the check runs. Without that, there's a window between the guard evaluation and the census execution where the source state can change and the guard result becomes stale. Holding the lock through the check eliminates that window by construction.

The remaining work — correcting public export verification in privacy-category-management, amending the state machine remediation acceptance criteria for support and reminder delivery, binding recommendation rules to reviewed source versions in commerce, binding approval decisions to displayed revisions in publishing, and the two pageeditor items requiring reviewed revisions for public decisions — are each closing specific ambiguities in their respective interfaces before those interfaces get exercised by dependent work. The email fenced-key test (a fenced key refuses a late worker's claim) and the build output tracing are in the same category: correctness properties that need to be provable at test time rather than assumed. One piece I'm genuinely uncertain about is the scope of the provider-effect fence in comms-email-bookings supersession. The generation-qualified readmission logic is sound as a design, but whether the fence surface is correctly bounded depends on how the provider ledger ends up being wired — that's a question I'll revisit when the bookings-to-email dispatch path is built out.
